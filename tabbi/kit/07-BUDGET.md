# Money and time — honest estimates

Everything below is an estimate with stated assumptions. Ad rates vary enormously
by month and by audience mix, and platform rules change. Treat the ranges as
planning figures, not forecasts, and verify the thresholds yourself on the
platforms — they move.

## What it costs to build

| item | cost |
|---|---|
| rendering, encoding, hosting the source | **€0** — your machine, ffmpeg, git |
| fonts, assets, stock, music licences | **€0** — everything is drawn and synthesised in code |
| voice generation (if you ever add it) | **€0** — Qwen3-TTS is Apache 2.0, runs on your laptop |
| Claude subscription | **€17–90 / month** depending on plan |
| domain, if you want one | **€10–15 / year** |

**Realistic monthly cash cost: €20–100.** That is the whole thing. Your real
cost is time.

## What it costs in time

Now that the engine exists:

| task | time |
|---|---|
| new 5-min episode (writing + framing passes) | 4–8 hours of agent session |
| full render | ~32 min, unattended |
| cutting 5 Shorts from an episode | ~30 min |
| thumbnail | ~10 min, generated in code |
| upload + metadata | ~15 min per video |

**Roughly one episode plus five Shorts per week is sustainable** alongside a
day job, which is the cadence `05-GROWTH.md` argues for.

## What it might earn — and when

### Monetisation thresholds (verify current rules on YouTube — these change)
- 1,000 subscribers **and** 4,000 public watch hours in 12 months, **or**
- 1,000 subscribers **and** 10 million valid public Shorts views in 90 days

Note the second route: 10M Shorts views sounds enormous, but a single Short
that travels can do 2–5M on its own. Shorts is the faster path for you.

### Revenue, once monetised

Assumptions: English-language animated comedy, audience spread across US,
India, Brazil, Philippines, Indonesia, Europe. **Not** a US-dominant channel —
that is the single biggest factor and it cuts rates significantly.

| format | realistic RPM | 1M views ≈ |
|---|---|---|
| long-form (5 min) | €1.00–4.00 per 1,000 | €1,000–4,000 |
| Shorts | €0.03–0.15 per 1,000 | €30–150 |

**Shorts monetise badly. Long-form monetises.** That is exactly why the
strategy is Shorts for reach, long-form for revenue. The Shorts are marketing
that happens to pay a little.

### A plausible six-month picture

I am giving you three scenarios because a single number would be dishonest —
outcomes here are dominated by whether one video breaks out, which is mostly
luck multiplied by volume.

| | month 3 | month 6 |
|---|---|---|
| **nothing breaks out** | ~500 subs, €0 | ~2k subs, not yet monetised, €0 |
| **one Short travels** | ~15k subs, €0 | monetised, €150–600 / month |
| **format clicks** | ~60k subs, monetised | €800–3,000 / month |

**The honest base case is the first row.** Most channels live there. Plan your
finances as if you will earn nothing for six months, and treat anything else as
upside. If you need income sooner, this is not the vehicle — your freelance dev
work is.

## Where the money actually is, if this works

Not AdSense. AdSense is the proof, not the business. The real outcomes for a
channel like this, in rough order of likelihood:

1. **Brand deals** — a single integration for a 50k-subscriber animation
   channel is typically worth more than a month of ad revenue.
2. **The engine itself** — you now own a working code-native animation pipeline.
   That is a product, or a service, independently of the channel.
3. **Merch** — only ever works with a character people are attached to. That is
   a year-two question, not a launch question.

## Things to verify yourself, do not take my word

- Whether TikTok's creator monetisation is available in Morocco at all
- That AdSense supports payout to your bank or a supported method in Morocco
- Current YouTube Partner Programme thresholds
- Whether a company already holds your chosen name as a trademark in
  entertainment (class 41)
