# Reaching a global audience from Rabat

## The thing to internalise first

**The algorithm does not know where you are and does not care.** It knows what
language your video is in, how long people watch it, and whether they finish
it. A channel in Rabat and a channel in Los Angeles get the same distribution
for the same retention curve.

What actually localises you is **content that requires local context.** That is
the only trap, and it is entirely under your control.

## The thesis that makes this work

You are not making Moroccan comedy or American comedy. You are making comedy
about **the one culture that is genuinely global: being annoyed at a website.**

A CAPTCHA works the same in Rabat, Lagos, Ohio and Jakarta. So does:

- a cancel button that moves
- a password rule you cannot satisfy
- a cookie banner with 400 "legitimate interest" toggles
- a delivery driver who followed the instructions exactly
- a 2FA code that arrives after it expires
- "your call is important to us"
- a form that clears itself when one field is wrong

Nobody needs a translation and nobody needs to know where you live. **That is
your unfair advantage, not a limitation you are working around.**

## How to tell if an idea is global

Ask: *would this still be funny to someone who has never heard of the company,
the country, or the language?* If any of those three is load-bearing, cut it.

Concretely, do not use:
- real brand names, logos, or anything that reads as one
- prices in dirhams, or any currency tied to one place (the episodes use €
  because it reads as "generic money" to most of the world)
- text in the frame that needs reading speed above a B1 English level
- references to local platforms, banks, or telecoms

## Language

English. Short lines, 2–9 words. Present tense. No idioms, no wordplay that
depends on English — the jokes must survive a viewer whose English is their
third language, because most of your audience's will be.

This is also why the no-voice decision is strategically right, not just
convenient: **on-screen text in simple English is understood by more of the
planet than spoken English is**, and it works on mute, which is how most of a
feed gets watched.

## Where the views will actually come from

For English-language animated comedy with a global premise, expect a spread
roughly like: United States, India, Brazil, Philippines, Indonesia, United
Kingdom, Germany, Mexico. Not one dominant country.

This matters for two reasons:
1. **Upload time should target the US evening**, because that is the single
   largest block and it seeds the first hour of distribution. 17:00 UTC is
   13:00 US East and 18:00 in Rabat — convenient for you, correct for them.
2. **Your RPM will be lower than a US-only channel's**, because ad rates in
   India, Brazil and Indonesia are a fraction of US rates. Budget for that.
   It is in `07-BUDGET.md`.
