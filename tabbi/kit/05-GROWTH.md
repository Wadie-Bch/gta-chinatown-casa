# Growth: what to measure and what good looks like

## The strategic call: launch with Shorts, not with the five-minute film

This is the most important page in the kit.

A five-minute animated video from a channel with zero subscribers is the
hardest possible thing to get distributed. Nobody has a reason to give you five
minutes. Meanwhile the same jokes cut to 25 seconds are exactly what the Shorts
feed is built to push to strangers.

**So: the five-minute episodes are your library, not your launch.** Cut each
one into 4–6 Shorts, post the Shorts, and let the Shorts sell the long-form.

The good news is the episodes are already built out of self-contained gags —
the CAPTCHA "TOO CORRECT" beat, the accordion inside the accordion, the ticket
counting up while the board counts down. Each one is a Short with a beginning
and a punchline.

## The only two numbers that matter early

**1. Retention at 3 seconds.** If people leave in the first three seconds,
nothing else you do matters. Open on the problem, in motion, with the funniest
frame you have. Never open on a logo, a title card, or a slow zoom.

**2. Average view duration as a percentage.** Not views. Percentage watched.

| format | weak | okay | strong |
|---|---|---|---|
| Short (20–40 s) | under 50% | 60–80% | over 100% (rewatches) |
| Long (5 min) | under 30% | 40–50% | over 55% |

A Short over 100% means people are watching it twice. That is the signal that
makes a video travel.

## What to ignore early

Subscribers, likes, comments. They are outcomes, not levers. Chasing them
makes you write worse videos. Watch retention; subscribers follow.

## Realistic milestones for a new channel posting 3× a week

I want to be straight with you: **most channels see nothing for the first 15–25
videos.** That is not failure, it is the sample size the algorithm needs.

| point | realistic | what it means |
|---|---|---|
| videos 1–10 | 100–2,000 views each | you are calibrating; look only at retention |
| videos 10–25 | one video does 10–50× the others | that is your format. make more of that one. |
| month 3 | 1k–20k subs *if* one video broke out | wildly variable, mostly luck × volume |
| month 6 | monetisation threshold plausible | see `07-BUDGET.md` |

If after 25 videos nothing has 60%+ retention, the problem is the videos, not
the algorithm. Change the format, not the posting schedule.

## Cadence

Three Shorts a week beats one every day that you cannot sustain, and it beats
one long video a week with nothing between. The engine makes the marginal cost
of a Short nearly zero once the episode exists — use that.

## Titles and thumbnails

For Shorts: no thumbnail. The first frame is the thumbnail. Make frame one the
funniest composition in the clip, not a build-up to it.

For long form: the thumbnail is 80% of the click. Four words maximum, one
emotion on the character's face, one saturated block behind. The one in this
repo already follows that.
